Banking and money transfers ​

Qatar Central Bank (QCB) supervises the banks operating in Qatar,[1] and it has a department that handles customer complaints. This page covers the basics for anyone who lives in Qatar and uses a bank here.[2]

Opening a bank account ​

QCB's instructions to banks list what goes into a personal account file. They apply to residents and non-residents, but this edition is from September 2013, so ask your bank for its current checklist before you go.[3]

What you need to give the bank:[3]

  • A full copy of your valid passport or identification card.[3]
  • A personal photograph or fingerprints, as the instructions require.[3]
  • Your full name as shown on your passport or ID, your nationality, and your place and date of birth.[3]
  • Your profession, workplace, place of residence and postal address.[3]
  • If you live in Qatar, the name and address of your sponsor or employer.[3]
  • Your signature or fingerprint.[3]

Things to know before you apply:[3]

  • You normally have only one main account with a bank, though sub-accounts can sit under it.[3]
  • Before opening a current account, the bank must check you against the rules on returned cheques.[3]
  • Cheques cannot be used to draw money from a savings account.[3]

Getting your salary ​

The Wage Protection System is run with the Ministry of Labour to manage and verify wage payments from employers to employees. Its purpose and your rights under it are covered in Work and labour rights.[4]

Sending money inside Qatar ​

  • Fawran is the instant payment system, launched in 2024. It moves money between bank accounts at any time of day, and you can pay someone using their mobile number, alias or IBAN.[4]
  • Participating banks listed by QCB include QNB, Commercial Bank, QIB, Ahli Bank, Dukhan Bank, Doha Bank, QIIB, Al Rayan Bank, HSBC and Standard Chartered.[4]
  • Tahweel is the older system for transfers between bank accounts at banks in Qatar.[4]
  • Qatar Mobile Payment (QMP) handles instant transfers between e-wallets with payment service providers in Qatar.[4]

Exchange houses ​

QCB's instructions define an exchange house as a licensed company that exchanges money and does not take deposits.[5] QCB's Licensing Section handles the licences of the institutions it supervises, including renewals and cancellations.[6]

Complaining about a bank ​

QCB's Customer Protection Department receives customer complaints and says it deals with them fairly and impartially.[2]

  • Complaints line for financial institutions: 8000700.[2]
  • Complaints line for insurance companies: 44456377.[2]
  • Email: cp@qcb.gov.qa.[2]
  • In person: Qatar Central Bank, West Bay, 8:00 AM to 1:00 PM.[2]

Avoiding fraud ​

QCB's Stay Aware campaign says scammers may pretend to be your bank, and that the main channels are phone calls, social media, email, SMS and links.[7]

  • Never share your card details or one-time password (OTP).[7]
  • Do not answer unknown international calls, and be careful with unknown local callers.[7]
  • Avoid opening links in SMS messages, and check who the sender really is.[7]
  • If a call seems fraudulent, contact the organisation directly using its official channels.[7]
  • If your card is lost or stolen, block it straight away through your bank's call centre or app.[7]

To report financial cybercrime, call the hotline on 66815757, use the Metrash2 app, or email cccc@moi.gov.qa.[7]
