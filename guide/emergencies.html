Emergencies and important numbers ​

In an emergency, call 999. Save the numbers on this page in your phone now, and teach them to your family.[1]

999 ​

The Ministry of Interior's Emergency Services Section takes criminal, fire and medical emergency calls on 999, around the clock. It has staff who can translate several languages, including English, French, Chinese, Filipino and Urdu.[2] It also passes accident reports to the right authority.[2]

When you call, give your name, your phone number, the nature of the emergency and your exact location.[3] Speak clearly, answer the operator's questions and follow the instructions you are given.[4]

Ambulance ​

Call 999 for a condition that needs immediate medical help to save a life, such as stroke, chest pain, a severe allergic reaction, a seizure or difficulty breathing.[4] Hamad Medical Corporation (HMC) says people with conditions that are not life-threatening should not dial 999, and should use urgent care if it cannot wait.[4]

If you are deaf ​

The Ministry of Interior runs an Emergency Service for the Deaf on 992, around the clock. The ministry's page limits it to deaf users.[5] You can reach it in three ways:

  • A video call to 992 from a computer or mobile phone.[5]
  • An SMS to 992.[5]
  • An email to 992@moi.gov.qa.[5]

In an SMS, state clearly the problem and the service you need, such as ambulance, civil defence or police, plus your name, your address and any injuries or deaths.[5] SMS can be delayed by the mobile network, so do not rely on it alone. Make sure your message has arrived by contacting the police.[5] Misuse of this service can lead to legal action.[5]

Finding emergency care ​

For a life-threatening emergency, call 999 or go to the nearest HMC Emergency Department.[1]

  • Adults: Hamad General Hospital Trauma and Emergency Center, Al Wakra, Al Khor, Hazm Mebaireek General, Aisha Bint Hamad Al Attiyah and The Cuban Hospital.[1]
  • Pregnancy and gynaecology emergencies: Women's Wellness and Research Center.[1]
  • Children under 14: the Pediatric Emergency Centers at Al Sadd, Airport, Al Rayyan, Al Wakra, Al Khor and Aisha Bint Hamad Al Attiyah Hospital.[1]
  • Urgent but not life-threatening: the Primary Health Care Corporation (PHCC) runs 16 Urgent Care Units, open 24 hours, seven days a week. You need no appointment.[1]

If you are not sure where to go, call 16000. Choose option 2 then option 2 for PHCC urgent consultation, or option 3 then option 1 for HMC urgent consultation, which runs 8 am to 3 pm, Sunday to Thursday.[1]

After a road accident ​

If anyone is seriously hurt, call 999 for an ambulance.[4]

For a minor accident between two vehicles, you can register it in the Metrash2 app instead of going to traffic investigation in person:[6]

  1. Take up to 4 photos of each vehicle, with one clearly showing the number plate.[6]
  2. Move the vehicles away from the scene and park off the road.[6]
  3. Turn on location services on your phone.[6]
  4. In Metrash2, open Traffic Services, then Accident Report.[6]
  5. Enter both vehicles' details: vehicle number, QID number and mobile number.[6]
  6. Attach the photos, confirm the data is correct and submit.[6]
  7. Wait for the text messages. After traffic investigators review the photos, both drivers are told to go to the insurance company or are given approval for the repair.[6]

The Ministry of Interior also lists printing traffic accident reports among its traffic services, for repairing damaged vehicles.[7]

Power or water emergency ​

Kahramaa treats it as an emergency when your power or water is cut without prior warning from Kahramaa.[8] Call 991 at any hour, or email 991@km.com.qa.[8] Have your electricity and water number ready, because the phone system needs it.[9]

Quick list ​

  • 999: police, fire and medical emergencies.[2]
  • 992: emergency service for deaf people.[5]
  • 991: Kahramaa electricity and water emergencies.[9]
  • 4439 6066: Pediatric Emergency Center hotline, 24 hours.[1]
  • 16000: National Healthcare Helpline.[1]
  • 107: PHCC, including urgent or same day appointments.[10]
  • 16060: HMC Nesma'ak Patient Contact Center, 24 hours.[11]
