Public holidays and Ramadan working hours ​

This page covers the paid holidays private-sector workers are entitled to, the shorter hours during Ramadan, and how to behave respectfully during the month. The holiday and hours rules come from the National Human Rights Committee's Worker's Pocket Booklet of December 2021, so check for later changes.[1]

Paid holidays in the private sector ​

The booklet lists these days as holidays with full pay:[1]

  • Eid al-Fitr: three days.[1]
  • Eid al-Adha: three days.[1]
  • National Day: one day, on 18 December.[1]
  • Three more days, with dates set by your employer.[1]

If the work requires you to work on any of these days, you must be compensated.[1]

National Sport Day ​

Qatar Central Bank's notice for 2024 called National Sport Day, Tuesday 13 February 2024, an official holiday, with financial institutions reopening the next day. Check each year's notice for the date, and ask your employer whether it applies to you, because the booklet's list does not name this day.[2]

How holiday dates reach you ​

Qatar Central Bank posts its own notices for financial institutions on its website, such as its National Day circular for 2024 and its National Sport Day notice for 2024. Use these to see when banks will close.[3]

Working hours in Ramadan ​

  • Private sector: the booklet gives six working hours a day during Ramadan, with a break that does not count as working time.[1]
  • Banks and other financial institutions: for Ramadan 2026 (1447 AH), Qatar Central Bank set five hours a day, from 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM. This can change from year to year.[4]

Ramadan starts with the sighting of the crescent moon and lasts about 29 or 30 days, so the dates change every year.[5]

During Ramadan ​

Visit Qatar gives this advice for visitors, and it is a sensible guide for new residents too.[5]

  • Avoid eating, drinking or smoking in public during daylight fasting hours.[5]
  • Avoid loud music and offensive language.[5]
  • Dress modestly in public places. Light clothing that covers the shoulders and knees is generally recommended.[5]
  • Check opening times before you go out, as attractions, restaurants and shops may change their hours.[5]
  • If you are not fasting, many hotels, restaurants and designated dining areas still serve food.[5]
