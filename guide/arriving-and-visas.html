Arriving and visas ​

How you enter Qatar depends on your nationality and your reason for coming. Visitors may get a visa on arrival or apply online through Hayya, while people moving here come in on a residence visa and finish their residency steps after landing.[1][2]

The Ministry of Interior (MOI) says visitor visa rules can change without notice, so check the rules for your nationality close to your travel date.[1]

Ways to enter ​

Visa on arrival or no visa in advance ​

  • MOI says citizens of more than 95 countries can enter with a visa on arrival at Qatar's entry points, and that citizens of several countries are exempt from getting a visa beforehand.[1]
  • The length of stay varies by nationality.[1] MOI's on-arrival page lists tourist categories of 90 days and 30 days, along with other categories such as a visa for GCC residents and a business visa with its own terms.[3]
  • GCC nationals do not need a visa. Hayya says your passport or GCC ID is enough.[4]

Hayya e-visas ​

  • Hayya is the online platform for tourist visas. MOI lists four entry permits on it: the tourist visa, the entry visa for residents of GCC countries, the entry visa for people accompanying GCC citizens, and entry by Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA).[1]
  • You apply at hayya.qa or through the Hayya mobile app.[1][4]

Visas issued in advance ​

  • These are visas you receive before you travel and present at the arrival port. MOI's list includes business visas and family (personal visit) visas, which have their own page: Family visas.[5]

Coming to live or work ​

  • MOI's Work Visa on Company Sponsorship is for workers being brought to ministries, government bodies, semi-government organizations and private companies.[6]
  • MOI lists these conditions: attested educational certificates, an employment contract between you and the employer, a good conduct certificate from your home country attested by the Qatar Embassy there, and passing the medical test outside Qatar.[6]
  • If you enter on a residence visa, you must do the medical checkup within thirty days of entering. Fingerprints come after the medical, and then the residency procedures are completed. The Residence permit and QID page has the details.[2]

What to have ready at the border ​

  • A valid passport. The two MOI pages differ on how long it must stay valid: the visit visas page says at least three months, and the visa conditions page says not less than 6 months.[1][7] Having six months or more meets both.[7]
  • A confirmed return ticket or a confirmed ticket to continue your journey.[1]
  • Valid health insurance from a company registered with the Ministry of Public Health.[1]
  • For some nationalities, a hotel reservation, cash or a credit card.[7]
  • If you are driving in, vehicle insurance. Visitors using the Abu Samra border complete the required policies electronically and can pre-register through Hayya.[1]

How to check a visa ​

  • MOI's Visa Services page lets you follow the approval of a work or visit visa with the application number, or look it up with the visa number, and print the details of an issued visa.[8]
  • The same page has a Track Application Status option for the Qatar Visa Center.[8]
  • For a Hayya e-visa, use Track your E-Visa with your entry reference number, or your passport number and nationality.[9]

Staying longer and overstaying ​

  • Confirm how long your visit visa allows when you enter. MOI says a family visit gives you one month.[2]
  • To stay longer on a family visit, finish the medical checkup before your allowed period ends and apply for an extension.[2]
  • If the visa runs out without an extension, MOI charges a fine for each day of delay, up to a maximum. The amounts are on the MOI tips page.[2]
  • The Visa Services page also has a Visa Extension / Fine Payment option.[8]
  • You can look up overstay violations through the Overstay Violations Inquiry service.[10]
